## Bugs (most severe first)

1. PRIV, phone learns from fields it shouldn't (Typist.kt:695, 420, 932, 959). `learn()` is gated only by `noLearn`, so email/URL fields and single-line NO_SUGGESTIONS fields (usernames, codes, letters of an OTP) feed user-<lang>.json, the nightly Mac backup, and come back as suggestions/predictions. Gate on `noCorrect` too (or reuse `aiField`). Sure.
2. PRIV, user word store grows forever (core/src/typing.rs:403-455). Every non-dictionary token typed once (typos, names, IDs) is kept in `user.words`, and every previous word gets a `user.next` entry, with no cap or ageing (`used` is capped, these aren't). Kept on disk and backed up indefinitely; it also slows item 6. Prune entries under ACCEPT that haven't been seen for N days, and cap `next` keys. Sure.
3. PRIV, postal addresses and names go to the AI (core/src/text.rs:264-273). `ai_allowed` doesn't block POSTAL_ADDRESS (0x70) or PERSON_NAME (0x60); PLAN says nothing typed in an address goes to the AI. Add both, and FILTER/PHONETIC too. Sure.
4. PRIV, typed text can reach `keys log` through crash logging (App.kt:30, 38, 115). These log `e.toString()`. A Rust panic surfaced by UniFFI carries its message, and Rust's str-slice panic quotes the string (up to 256 chars). Log the exception class and stack frames only. Latent (it needs a panic on typed text); fairly sure.
5. Held dictation loses its end (learn/Voice.kt:143-149 against 130-139). `release()` stops the recorder and copies `buffer` while the IO loop may still be in `read()` or about to write its last chunk (buffer ≥ 0.5 s). The last word can be cut before Whisper hears it. Join the reader job before `toByteArray()`. Fairly sure.
6. Phone jank: disk writes under the engine lock (core/src/lib.rs:478-485). `Engine::save` serialises and writes every user-<lang>.json while holding the state mutex. Typist calls it every 25 words, and the main thread's next `suggestIn`/`knowsWord` waits on that lock. Serialise under the lock, write after. The lock is certain; the size of the stall is unmeasured.
7. Mac preview can die until relaunch (desktop/src/apple.rs:35, worker.rs:109-157, meaning.rs:83). Helper replies are read with a blocking `read_line` and no deadline: a hung translator freezes the worker for good. A helper restart happens only on "Broken pipe", and a `start()` that returns None once (for example while an update swaps the bundle) is never retried. Fairly sure.
8. Mac screen words come straight back after timing out (desktop/src/float.rs:447-449). A word that times out after 3 min of use isn't graded. If it was due, `Review::next` sorts it first and it's no longer in `up` (it's in `leaving`), so it comes back about 4 s later. Rest it, or count the timeout as a soft Again. Fairly sure (from reading).
9. PRIV, Mac caches spoken lines (desktop/src/speak.rs:195-207). `say()` saves a file for anything spoken, so every preview line read aloud (a translation of what was typed) is stored as audio in ~/Library/Caches for 14 days. It's also passed to `say` as argv, which `ps` shows. Cache single screen words only. Sure; docs/mac.md mentions it, but PLAN's owner's-call list doesn't.
10. PRIV, clipboard history keeps secrets (KeysService.kt:155-157, learn/Clips.kt:40). History keeps anything not flagged EXTRA_IS_SENSITIVE: API keys, and codes copied from apps that don't flag them. Skip text where `looksSecret` is true (the chip can still show it). Sure; low-medium.
11. Wrong space before flags and joined emoji (Typist.kt:1020). The kaomoji test is `text.length > 2` (UTF-16), so flags (🇫🇷 is 4 units), skin tones and ZWJ emoji get a space before them. Count graphemes, or check against the kaomoji list. Sure.
12. Mac main-thread freezes on a right-click swap (desktop/src/main.rs:298-358). `ax::replace_range` sleeps 270 ms and can wait up to 0.5 s per AX timeout, all on the main thread. Move it to a thread like chatswap's Swapper. Sure; low.
13. `keys word` / `keys forget` break on phrases (Control.kt:93, 105; cli/src/main.rs:34-36). The arg is split on spaces, so "parvenir à" reads "à" as a status. Treat the last token as a status only when it's new/learning/known. Sure; low.
14. Mac never retries missed dictionaries (desktop/src/learn.rs:96-116). The SHA256SUMS fetch happens once at launch, and launch is at login, often before Wi-Fi is up. If it fails, no missing dictionary installs until the next launch. All 24 learning languages' dictionaries also download before the first word sync. Fairly sure; low.
15. Small ones (low):
    - Strip.kt `index/pin/typing/moved/source` are written on Main and from `show()` on IO with no synchronisation; `flags[index]` (l.297) reads after a suspension.
    - Helper `Child`s are dropped without kill or wait, leaving zombies or hung helpers (apple.rs, meaning.rs).
    - gloss.rs:12-35 `form_of` slices the original at a byte offset found in its `to_lowercase()`; it panics if a char before " of " changes byte length.
    - Updater.kt:113 compares signer certs by `contentHashCode()`; use the bytes (Android's own check covers it today).
    - Voice.kt:81 silently falls back to Google's online recogniser in no-learning fields.
    - Pre-API-33 `getExtractedText(hintMaxChars=0)` copies the whole field on every selection change (Typist.kt:1159).
16. Repo junk: a stray PNG named `--closed` at the repo root (commit 0544568). It's a `--word-picture` render of a scratchpad path. Public repo; `git rm -- --closed`. Sure.

## Refactors / modernising

1. One dictionary installer in the core. Download → SHA256SUMS → install → ipadic unzip exists twice: Kotlin `Dictionaries` (learn/Learning.kt:123-227) and Rust desktop/src/learn.rs. The core already has reqwest and tokio: add `Engine::ensure_dictionary(lang)` (with retry/backoff, fixing bug 14), and Android calls it off-thread.
2. One Swift-helper client (desktop). apple.rs `Helper` and meaning.rs `Helper` duplicate the spawn plus the JSON-lines protocol. Make one `HelperClient::request(json, deadline)` that kills and respawns on timeout or EOF (fixes 7 and the zombies).
3. Mac: read only the tail of the field (desktop/src/ax.rs:290-303). Every 150 ms it copies the whole AXValue (a long Notes or Docs body) and re-encodes it to UTF-16 to keep about 300 chars. Use `AXStringForRange` over [caret-400, caret], and fall back to AXValue.
4. Core AI calls: `correct/translate_ai/gloss/ask` repeat the provider lookup, `block_on(ask)` and error mapping, and `ask` re-reads `ai.provider` (lib.rs:203-206, 700-776). One `ask_ai(messages, key, thinking)` covers them.
5. `word_face` (lib.rs:108-173) calls `self.learn.card()` up to about 8 times for the same word (each one is a dictionary lookup plus a status walk). It runs on the Mac main thread for up to 4 picks per new screen word. Memoise per call.
6. Workspace `[profile.release] opt-level = "z"` (Cargo.toml) also slows the desktop app (autocorrect, layout, AX tree walks) for no size gain. Set "z" only for the Android build (a profile override for ccez-keys-core under cargo-ndk, or a separate `android` profile).
7. Kotlin threading: Strip/Tips/Ask hop between `app.scope` (IO) and `withContext(Main)` with shared mutable fields. Keep their state on Main and push only translate/AI/dictionary calls to IO (structured: one `CoroutineScope(Main.immediate)` per keyboard session, cancelled in `finish()`). This also removes the per-token `scope.launch(Main)` in Ask's AnswerSink.
8. Small modernising:
   - `getParcelableExtra<Intent>` → `IntentCompat.getParcelableExtra` (Updater.kt:145).
   - Wrap the Mac spot thread's loop (float.rs:267) in `autoreleasepool`: it calls NSWorkspace/Foundation forever on a std thread.
   - `std::mem::transmute` of NSDictionary generics (idle.rs:209) → objc2's checked cast.
   - Fully qualified `kotlinx.coroutines.*` / `uk.ccez.keys.learn.*` all through Typist.kt → imports.

## Features (fit: no decks, FSRS inside what he already does, pithy)

1. Typing a Learning word counts as a review. When he types a word that's Learning, correctly and in its language (phone strip languages, or Mac fields), grade it Good in the core's FSRS (word and language only, never the sentence, and never in no-learning fields). His own writing does the reviewing; screen words then skip what he's clearly using.
2. Mac preview in whatever language he types. `from` is fixed to "en" (preview.rs:171). He types French, German, Japanese and Chinese too: detect the sentence's language (the Swift helper's NLLanguageRecognizer, or the core's `detect_language` with word lists loaded). Then show his other languages, plus English when it's a learning language, as the phone strip does.
3. Corrections come back as screen words. Accepted AI fixes (`mistakes.jsonl`) go into the FSRS schedule as screen words: the corrected word or phrase, with the wrong form small under it on hover. It reuses screen words, so there's no new screen.
4. One example on hover. After the tags on a hovered screen word, show one short Tatoeba sentence containing it (the data is already used for `data/next`), with the word bold and its translation under it, faded in like the tags. Context without a card.
5. Learning words lit in what he reads on the Mac. The AX walk for free spots already sees the front window's text runs. Underline his Learning words in French, German, Japanese or Chinese pages in their language colour, and let a hover give the screen-word face. Nothing is stored or sent, only matched against his list.
