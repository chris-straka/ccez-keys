ccez-keys

Eight screens from one layout. The learning strip sits above the suggestions and only appears while you type. Everything else is a normal keyboard.

Gestures

What each touch does

  • Swipe the space bar: the next language you type in. Hold it: the list.
  • Two words in another language you type: it switches on its own (dot on the space bar).
  • Tap a word in the strip: its card. Hold a word: Learning, no card.
  • Tap the flag, or swipe the strip: next language you're learning. 🔊 reads it aloud.
  • Hold the strip: send the translation instead of what you typed.
  • ✦ on a tip, card or copied message: chat with Muse about it.
  • Swipe a clipboard chip away to dismiss it; hold it for the history.
  • Hold a suggestion: Learning in every language shown under it.
  • Password fields: no strip, no AI, nothing learned or kept. Everywhere else Muse reads along.
How it's built

Rust underneath, a thin Android shell

  • Swipe typing is in settings, off at first. No limit on languages (SwiftKey stops at six).
  • Kotlin keyboard service; keys drawn on one canvas so a tap shows in the same frame. Panels in Compose. No Tauri or web view: they lag, and Android closes heavy keyboards.
  • Rust core through UniFFI, like ccez-tube: autocorrect, next word, language detection, kaomoji ranking, settings, logs.
  • Dictionaries, furigana, pinyin, word statuses, ccez-words sync and the Muse client move out of ccez-tube into one shared crate both apps use.
  • Live translation with ML Kit on the phone (instant, offline). Muse for tips and better wording. Gemini Nano on the phone as the private option, as in ccez-llm.
  • Clipboard on ccez-clip's core, kept on the phone only. Dictation: Android's speech recogniser for live words, ccez-whisper for a cleaner pass when you stop.
  • keys on the Mac, like clip: every setting, languages, buttons, words and logs, over USB or the tailnet.
Order

What gets built first

  1. A keyboard you can use every day: QWERTY with accents for English, French, German and Spanish, autocorrect, space-bar switching, the button row, the clipboard chip, settings, keys.
  2. The learning strip: translation, furigana and pinyin, sound, cards, Learning and Known from ccez-words, words under suggestions.
  3. Emoji, kaomoji that know the app, clipboard history.
  4. Japanese (romaji to kana and kanji) and Chinese (pinyin to hanzi). The hardest piece: each needs a converter that picks the right characters.
  5. Dictation, Muse tips, Ask Muse, switching language on its own.
  6. Arabic, then any other layout a new language needs (Hangul, Hindi, AZERTY, QWERTZ).
  7. Later: words you met in ccez-tube this week come first in the strip; a quick review card when the keyboard opens; send selected text to ccez-llm to annotate.
